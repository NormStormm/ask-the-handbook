# Kaimana Demo Resort: Food & Beverage Team Handbook

*Sample handbook for a fictitious resort, written for a software demo. It is not a real company's policy and is not legal advice.*

## 1.1 Welcome and who to ask
Welcome to the Food & Beverage team at Kaimana Demo Resort. Your direct supervisor is the outlet manager on your schedule (Lanai Grill, Pool Bar, Banquets or In-Room Dining). For questions about pay, benefits, time off balances or paperwork, contact Human Resources (HR) at extension 4100, Monday to Friday, 8 am to 4:30 pm. For anything urgent outside those hours, ask the Manager on Duty (MOD), reachable through the front desk at extension 0.

## 1.2 Your first day
Report to the HR office (lower lobby, next to the employee entrance) at 8:30 am. Bring two forms of ID for your I-9 paperwork, your bank details for direct deposit, and closed-toe, non-slip shoes. HR will issue your employee ID badge, locker assignment and uniform. Your manager will meet you at 10:30 am for a property tour. Lunch is provided in the employee cafeteria. Your first day is paid at your regular rate.

## 1.3 Your first week
During your first week you will: complete the online food safety and allergen courses (about 2 hours, paid); shadow a trainer for at least three shifts before working alone; learn the POS system and get your POS login; read the emergency procedures (section 6.1); and sign the handbook acknowledgment form. Your manager will check in with you at the end of week one.

## 1.4 Your first month
By the end of your first 30 days: pass the menu and allergen knowledge check (80% to pass; you may retake it once); complete the guest service training "LEARN" (section 5.1); and have a 30-day review with your manager. Employees in alcohol-serving positions must also complete responsible alcohol service certification within 30 days of hire; the resort pays the course fee.

## 2.1 Schedules
Schedules are posted every Wednesday by 5 pm for the following Monday-Sunday week, in the scheduling app and on the board outside the kitchen office. Check your schedule before every shift; changes after posting are sent through the app. If you are not scheduled for a week, you are still employed: talk to your manager about hours.

## 2.2 Shift swaps
You may swap a shift with a coworker in the same position. Post the swap in the scheduling app; it only counts once your manager approves it. Until it is approved, you are still responsible for the original shift. Swaps that would put either person over 40 hours in a week need manager approval because of overtime.

## 2.3 Calling out sick or late
If you cannot work a shift, call the outlet manager's line at least 4 hours before your start time (2 hours for morning shifts starting before 8 am). Texting a coworker is not a call-out. If you are running late, call before your start time. Two no-call/no-shows within 90 days may lead to termination.

## 2.4 Clocking in and out
Clock in and out at the time clock by the employee entrance using your badge. Do not clock in more than 7 minutes before your scheduled start without manager approval. Never clock in or out for another employee. If you forget to clock in or out, tell your manager the same day so they can fix the punch; do not wait until payday.

## 2.5 Meal and rest breaks
Employees working 6 or more hours receive one unpaid 30-minute meal break, scheduled by the manager. Employees also receive one paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked. Employees under 18 must take a 30-minute meal break after 5 hours of work. Clock out for meal breaks; do not clock out for rest breaks.

## 2.6 Overtime
Overtime is paid at 1.5 times your regular rate for hours over 40 in a Monday-Sunday workweek. All overtime must be approved by your manager in advance. Working unapproved overtime is still paid, but may lead to corrective action.

## 3.1 Pay schedule
Pay is every other Friday by direct deposit. Pay stubs are available in the HR portal. If your paycheck looks wrong, contact HR within 7 days of payday.

## 3.2 Tips and service charges
Servers and bartenders keep their own tips. Bussers, food runners and hosts receive a share of the tip pool: servers contribute 3% of their food and beverage sales, and bartenders contribute 2% of their sales. The pool is paid out with your paycheck. Banquet service charges are distributed according to the banquet service charge policy posted in the banquet office. Managers never take part in the tip pool.

## 3.3 Benefits
Full-time employees (averaging 30 or more hours a week) are eligible for medical, dental and vision coverage starting the first of the month after 30 days of employment. Part-time employees are not eligible for medical coverage but may join the retirement savings plan after 90 days. All employees receive a free meal per shift in the employee cafeteria and discounted parking (section 7.2).

## 4.1 Paid time off (PTO)
Full-time employees earn 1 hour of PTO for every 30 hours worked, starting on day one; PTO can be used after 90 days. Part-time employees earn PTO at the same rate but can carry over at most 40 hours into the next year. Unused PTO is not paid out when you leave unless the law requires it.

## 4.2 Requesting time off
Request time off in the scheduling app at least 3 weeks before the date. Requests are approved first-come, first-served, and may be denied during blackout periods: the week of Christmas through New Year's Day, Easter weekend, and the Fourth of July. You will get an answer in the app within 7 days of your request. Do not book travel until your request is approved.

## 4.3 Holidays
The resort is open every day of the year. Employees who work on New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving or Christmas Day are paid 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked that day.

## 5.1 Guest complaints: LEARN
Handle complaints with LEARN: Listen without interrupting; Empathize ("I'm sorry this happened"); Apologize; Resolve, or find someone who can; Notify your manager so they can follow up. You may offer a complimentary dessert or non-alcoholic drink up to $15 to resolve a complaint. Anything more, including comping a meal or a room charge, needs a manager.

## 5.2 Food allergies
Take every allergy seriously. When a guest mentions an allergy: write it on the ticket using the allergy button in the POS; tell the chef or sous chef in person, not only on the ticket; and use the allergen menu binder at the host stand to check ingredients. Never guess. If you are unsure whether a dish is safe, say so and ask the chef. If a guest has an allergic reaction, call security at extension 5555 immediately (section 6.2).

## 5.3 Responsible alcohol service
Check ID for anyone who looks under 40. Accept only valid, unexpired government photo IDs. Do not serve anyone who appears intoxicated; tell your manager, who will handle it. Never serve alcohol to an employee in uniform. The legal drinking age in Hawaii is 21.

## 5.4 Dress code
Wear the uniform issued by HR, clean and pressed, with your name tag. Black, closed-toe, non-slip shoes are required in all F&B areas. Hair longer than shoulder length must be tied back; kitchen staff must wear a hat or hairnet. Keep jewelry minimal: one ring and small stud earrings. No visible chewing gum. Visible tattoos are allowed if they are not offensive.

## 5.5 Phones
Keep personal phones in your locker or a pocket on silent while on the floor. Use your phone only on breaks and in the break room, never in guest view.

## 6.1 Emergencies
Fire: pull the nearest alarm, call extension 5555, and guide guests to the nearest exit, away from elevators. Meet at your outlet's assembly point (posted by each exit). Tsunami or other warnings: follow instructions from security and the MOD. Know where the fire extinguishers, first aid kit and AED are in your outlet during your first week.

## 6.2 Injuries and incidents
If a guest or coworker is hurt, call security at extension 5555 first; they are trained in first aid and will call 911 if needed. Do not move an injured person unless they are in danger. If you are injured at work, tell your manager right away, even if it seems minor, and complete an incident report before the end of your shift.

## 6.3 Harassment and reporting concerns
Harassment or discrimination of any kind is not tolerated. Report it to your manager, to HR, or anonymously through the Ethics Line at 1-800-555-0199 (a fictitious number for this sample). You will not be punished for reporting in good faith.

## 7.1 Lost key cards and badges
If you lose your employee badge, report it to security right away so it can be deactivated; a replacement from HR costs $10 after the first one. Guest room key cards found on the floor go to the front desk, never back to a guest directly.

## 7.2 Parking and transportation
Employees park in the employee lot on Example Beach Road, not in the guest garage. A monthly employee parking pass costs $25 through payroll deduction; sign up with HR. The resort reimburses half of a monthly bus pass; bring your receipt to HR.

## 7.3 Employee meals
One free meal is provided per shift in the employee cafeteria. Employees may not eat food from the kitchen, buffet or guest areas, including leftovers. Employees receive 25% off at resort restaurants when off duty, not in uniform.

## 7.4 Lockers
Lockers are assigned by HR. Use your own lock. The resort is not responsible for items left in lockers, and lockers may be inspected with notice. Empty your locker on your last day.
